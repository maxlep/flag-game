# Working notes

What the project is and where files live: see README.md. This file holds the rules that aren't visible in the code.

## Source of truth and targets

- This repo is canonical. `index.html` is a complete document; open it from disk to run the app. No build step, no bundler, no dependencies.
- Two deploy targets from the same files:
  - GitHub Pages: `.github/workflows/pages.yml` deploys `main` at the site root and the `preview` branch under `/preview/` (same site, so both can sit on a phone's home screen; the preview is renamed "Flags preview" and its stamp line says preview). Push to either branch to redeploy. Work meant for comparison goes on `preview`; merge to `main` when it's agreed.
  - The claude.ai artifact: it needs a bare fragment, not a full document. Generate it with `tools/artifact_fragment.py index.html` and publish that fragment together with `css/`, `js/` (including `js/map.js` and `js/data/map.js`), `flags-true/`, `icons/`, `favicon.ico` and `manifest.webmanifest` as supporting files. From `flags/` publish only the files with no `flags-true/` counterpart; the artifact holds at most 511 files.
- Keep both targets in sync: every change is published to the artifact and pushed to GitHub in the same step.
- Before each commit run `tools/stamp_version.py` and stage `js/version.js` and `index.html`: it writes the stamp and puts it on the asset links (`?v=`), so a fresh page never mixes old and new files. The home screen shows the time in small print, compares it with the `js/version.js` the site is serving, and offers a hard reload (a fresh `index.html` via `?r=`). GitHub Pages caches for about ten minutes, so a push can take that long to show.

## Data rules

- Country names: the official short name in the country's own preferred English form (Czechia, Türkiye, Eswatini, Timor-Leste, Côte d'Ivoire, Cabo Verde). Older or common names go in `ALIASES` in `js/data/names.js`, never in the display name.
- Descriptions (the `blurb` field): two or three plain sentences for a learner, no bullet points, legends labelled as legends, no superlatives ("the only flag that…") unless the Wikipedia flag article states them. Every description shipped so far has been checked against the English Wikipedia "Flag of …" article; keep that bar for edits and additions, and record what was verified. Blurbs are JavaScript string literals in double quotes, so they must not contain a double quote.
- Adoption years (`ADOPTED`, `ORIGIN` in `js/data/names.js`): `ADOPTED` is the latest visible change to the design, or the year it was made official; a law that only set shades or wrote down proportions already in use doesn't count, and a readopted flag keeps its readoption year. `ORIGIN` adds `[year, then, now]` where the same recognisable design is at least five years older: the year it first appeared and two labels of at most four words from the Wikipedia article saying what it was then and what changed ("21 stars", "27 stars"; "", "restored"); a label stays empty when the article doesn't say. One year, no chains; inspiration or shared colours don't count. Every year was checked against the Wikipedia infobox; the record and the rules are in `docs/factcheck-adopted.json`.
- Under a flag's name: the region beside it (`nameLine`, only where the set or round mixes regions or territories: `mixedRound()` in a round), then `facts()`: the capital on one line and the flag's years on the next, each its own link. Each year with its label (`.nw`, comma included) and the region label move to the next line whole when there is no room, and break inside only when longer than a line, so nothing runs past a narrow column.
- Capitals: the conventional choice; where disputed, say so in the field itself ("Sucre (constitutional), La Paz (seat of government)", "None").
- The 197 countries (193 UN members plus Vatican City, Palestine, Kosovo, Taiwan) are `sov: true`; everything else is a territory in the app's sense (territories, nations and regions, organisations, the Antarctic). `SETS` in `js/data/flags.js` only labels where a non-country row came from.
- Every non-country flag has a region in `GEO` (`js/data/names.js`): one of the five continents, "Antarctic" or "Organisations". `geoOf()` in `js/app.js` is the one place that decides a flag's region.
- Scope is one saved setting (`SET.scope`: countries, all, territories) shown on the home screen and the setup screen. It filters every region, the wide card ("Whole world", "Everything", "All territories"), study lists, weak spots and look-alikes; empty region cards are dropped. Rounds record the scope they ran under. Titles are one per scope, at region and world level: Lord (Countries), Warden (Territories), Emperor (All), each a full round on Master, typed, with no misses; earned ones show as marks on the cards whatever the current scope. Explorer stays the easy one: a full All-territories round at any difficulty.
- A flag with no design of its own (Northern Ireland, which flies the Union Jack) goes in `NOQUIZ` (`js/data/names.js`): it appears in study lists with a "not quizzed" pill and its `USE` line explains why, and it is never asked, never a distractor and never counted.
- Run `tools/check_data.py` before committing data or artwork changes. It fails on missing artwork, two flags with identical artwork, a `TRUER` ratio that disagrees with the file's viewBox, and a `VARIANTS` entry without its file.
- The map (`js/data/map.js`) is generated, never edited by hand. `tools/build_map.js` projects Natural Earth (public domain) with Equal Earth onto a 1000-wide canvas and writes, per set (world, and a Pacific-centred one for Oceania), the land outlines, one shape per flag, its centre and box, its capital, and a frame box per region. The header of the script says how to fetch Natural Earth and the npm packages it needs; the app itself needs neither. Rebuild after adding a flag that has a territory of its own (the script maps codes to Natural Earth features; the organisations have no shape). Borders are Natural Earth's de-facto drawing (Taiwan, Kosovo, Western Sahara, Crimea, Kashmir); capitals it can't match by name fall back to the shape's centre, and the build prints which. The build also keeps the small islands that simplification would shrink to points (São Tomé, Bioko, Cabo Verde, the Comoros; each simplified on its own to about 3 km, so a place's centre lands on land, unless that turns the ring inside out, which on a sphere covers the whole world: then the ring is kept as it was; the build stops if any outline still spans the map), merges a Spanish region's provinces into one outline, matches a capital by whole words and prefers one inside the place's outline (`CAPLL` gives the few the places file lacks: Tifariti for Western Sahara, inside the de-facto outline where Laayoune is not), and moves a capital that falls just off a simplified coast to just inside it; it prints each of these. Rebuilt with the same Natural Earth files and script, the output is identical byte for byte. `js/map.js` is the one place that draws a map: `mapSVG({code, frame})` frames the flag's region (`mapFrameFor`), widens the frame when the flag's centre lies outside it, and adds a dot for a place too small to see at that scale; every frame but World is drawn with the projection's vertical stretch (`MAP.ky`, by canvas y) corrected at its centre, so shapes look as they do on a globe; `fit` (an aspect ratio) frames the country closely instead, which is what the learn card uses, while the viewer shows the whole region and zooms.
- Areas (`AREAS` in `js/data/names.js`): the UN's sub-regions as Natural Earth gives them, in a geographic order within each region; every flag sits in exactly one, inside its own region (where the UN and the app disagree, the app's region wins: Cyprus is in Southern Europe). The comment above it lists the places set by hand. `tools/check_data.py` checks both. The study list and the map's list share one saved sort (`SET.sort`, `groupList`): A–Z (the default), Region (the world lists only; a region's list shows Area for it) or Area, as a pill beside the filter on the study list and at the top of the map's list. Grouped, the headers name the region or area and pin while their group scrolls by (`--sth`, `grpCover`), and the index down the side has the first letter of each header in order, repeats and all, its bubble naming the whole group (`groupIndex`). The index reads its letters on every move (the map's list redraws them on a new sort), and in a grouped list it jumps by a group's first entry, not its header: a header scrolled past stays pinned and reports the pinned place.
- The study list draws a small map under each flag (`wireMiniMaps`), only as entries scroll into view; drawing two hundred at once is too heavy.
- The explorable map (`mapExploreSVG`, opened from a region card's Map link (a card's actions are icons joined in one bordered control, in this order: quiz, which does what tapping the card does and fills in when the card is hovered; the book, the list; the map. The control is a group named for the set, and each button has a name, a tooltip and a description by `aria-describedby`; cards that aren't regions pass their own descriptions) or the study list's Map button; the region is picked from its title, `exRegion`, and a study list behind it follows) draws every flag of the region as its own shape, whatever the scope; the scope, which can be changed from the map's top bar (`exScope`; the same pill of three, `scopeChips`, sits under a study list's title, `studyScope`), only decides which are drawn plainly and which hatched (real lines clipped to those shapes and redrawn for the view, `lbHatch`, 6px apart on screen; a pattern fill gave uneven stripes), and a hatched one's card says it isn't in the scope. Land outside the region is grey. Larger shapes go first, so where they overlap the smaller is on top, but places in the scope always go above those outside it (in Countries the United Kingdom is plain, England is under it); a second tap on the same spot goes to the next one under it (`exTap`). Small places get a dot of fixed screen size with a wider tap ring: white with a dark ring and larger in the scope, small and dim grey outside it. Places outside the scope also get a paler fill under the hatch, and their card dims the flag and sets the "not in" pill in solid ink (the text stays at full strength). Beside the map is the study list of the places in the scope (`exListBuild`), with the letter index (`wireAlpha` with a box): on a phone a drawer at the bottom, on a wide screen a panel on the left. It has three states on one control, a tab on its edge over the map (`exStep`, drag or two arrows): hidden (`SET.exFold`, remembered), open, and the full study list, which is the drawer grown to fill the screen and then handed to the study view at the same place. The tab follows a drag on the window rather than capturing the pointer, which would send its clicks to the tab instead of its buttons. A tap on an entry selects the place and brings it into view (`exPick`: zoom only when it must, never closer than about 60 units across); a tap on the map outlines the entry and scrolls the list to it (smoothly); opening the list with a place selected shows it already there, with no motion. The up and down keys walk the list while it shows (`exArrow`), wrapping round at the ends, from the first entry in view when nothing in it is selected. A study entry's small map opens this same map with that place selected (`studyMap`), not a map of its own; the quiz card's map still opens the single-place map. The list never scrolls sideways. `showVariant` reaches the list and card as well as the page, so their design chips work. The list, card and title are insets on the map (`LB.ins`, `exVis`): the opening view centres the region in what shows, and panning may pull the map out from under them. The card (the same study entry) shows only when the list can't: folded, or a place outside the scope. An entry's flag opens the designs with no Map chip (`LB.noMap`), here and in the study list alike, since the entry's small map is the way to the map; and closing those returns to the map as it was (`EXRET`), list scroll included. The study list and the map are two views of one list and keep their place between them: the study list's Map button opens the drawer at the entry at the top of the page (`studyTop`), and expanding the list past open (`exToList`) opens the study list at the selected place, or at the entry at the top of the drawer. While the viewer is open the page behind is locked at the root (`html.locked`; a lock on the body doesn't reach the window, since the root clips overflow), and a new viewer takes its layout without transitions (`noanim`), so a redraw for a new scope doesn't slide the tab. The map is a page, not a pop-up: it has a back button and no ×: a square button left of the title, matching the zoom buttons, so the bar looks the same wherever the map was opened from; where it goes (`exBackTo`: the study list it came from, or Home) is in its tooltip and label, not on its face, which would repeat the region's name; the flag viewer keeps its ×. Nothing on it is scored. The plan and its open questions are in `docs/map-plan.md`.
- Look-alike sets (`js/data/groups.js`): each needs a `k` kind from the five used, and a hint that states a concrete visual difference for every flag in the set. Hints were fact-checked too.

## Adding or changing flag artwork

- Flags are always drawn in official proportions from `flags-true/` (svg-country-flags). `flags/` (flag-icons, 4:3) is only the fallback for the few flags with no true-proportion file; there is no setting to draw everything at 4:3, since that shows designs that don't exist.
- Variants: `VARIANTS` in `js/data/names.js` lists a flag's official designs for use on land (civil, state, reverse), each with Wikipedia's infobox "Use" label and a short difference. Entry 0 is `flags-true/<code>.svg`; the others are `flags-true/<code>-<kind>.svg`, with an optional ratio and `bare` flag for swallowtails. `USE` holds the infobox label for single-design flags where it isn't "National flag". Civil versions were derived from our artwork by dropping the arms; versions that add arms came from Wikimedia Commons (public domain). Check the infobox before adding one; ensigns and war flags don't count.
- Run `tools/snap_edges.py` on every new SVG, in both folders. It stops hairline seams between stripes.
- Add the true-proportion ratio to `TRUER` in `js/data/names.js` (width ÷ height from the SVG's viewBox). Non-rectangular flags go in `BARE`.
- A new flag also needs: an entry in `js/data/flags.js`, a Wikipedia title in `FLAGWIKI` if "Flag_of_<Name>" isn't the article, and a `CAPWIKI` entry if the capital's name is ambiguous on Wikipedia.

## Behaviour that the code relies on

- The quiz is a fixed-height screen (`.qscreen`), not a scrolling page: header, a middle band (`#qmid`) that scrolls on its own, and an answer drawer (`.qdrawer`) pinned at the bottom. Its height is `--vvh` and its offset `--vvt`, both set from `visualViewport` in `keyboard()`, so with a phone keyboard up the layout fits above it. The question flag is capped to the middle band. While a card is on screen the name options shrink to the right answer and the one picked, so the card gets the room; in Drill a correct answer shows no card and keeps every option, so the drawer's height doesn't change between answers. `.qscreen` has a transform, so a fixed-positioned descendant would position against it; the viewer is appended to `body` for that reason.
- In typed rounds the answer box is never replaced during a round: `render()` patches it in place (`patchAround`) so focus, and the phone keyboard, stay put.
- The Design setting (`SET.variant`: national, civil, state, random) picks which variant each question shows; questions store it as `q.v` (and `q.ov` per option) so a restored round looks the same. The info card, study list and viewer let the reader switch designs; that never changes the setting.
- Settings and progress live in localStorage under `flaglore.*` keys; the storage format must stay backward compatible (`rec()` migrates old records).
- Badges are awarded only in `checkBadges()` at round end and are never revoked.
- The round in progress is saved under `flaglore.round` on every answer and restored on load; `saveRound()` must run after any change to `S` during a round, and the key is cleared when leaving.
- Typed answers (Expert): exact → unique exact prefix/substring → typo-tolerant only when nothing matches exactly. Don't let fuzzy matches widen the "one suggestion left" rule; precise typists rely on it. On Check with several suggestions left, the first suggestion (the one shown first in the row) is what gets submitted. Master is exact names and aliases only, no suggestions.
- The viewer is the only zoom. It shows a flag's designs as slides (`openFlag(c,vi)`), its map alone (`openMap(c)`), or the explorable map of a study list (`openExplore(region)`), never two in one strip. A drag always pans; nothing swipes. The chip row (`State · Civil · Map`) and the arrow keys are the way across. The question flag opens it on a tap, a pinch or the wheel (`wireZoom`). A tap grows the flag from its place on the page to the viewer's full size and the viewer is then the ordinary one. A pinch or the wheel opens with the flag exactly where it is on the page (`from` rect) and hands the gesture's pointers over; that size is the floor, and pinching or zooming back down to it closes the viewer. The arrow keys walk the chips. Before the flag is named the viewer is `quiet`: no name, no caption, no chips. On the map slide the region sits beside the name, small and muted as on the cards, by the cards' rule (`whereShown`: in a round when it mixes regions, in a study list when the list does), and the line under it is the capital alone. Closing by any route refocuses the answer box if it had focus. There is no rest timer. The flag zooms by resizing the element (to 8x) so it stays sharp; the map zooms by its viewBox (to 80x), with the same gesture state mapped onto it in `lbApply`, and its markers shrunk back so they keep their size on screen. The map slide fills the stage and opens zoomed to the country's close frame (`data-fit`), so zooming out reaches the region and a double tap resets to it.
- Keyboard: Enter starts/continues, Esc goes back, Tab never leaves the page during a round. A swipe in from the left edge is also back (for the home-screen app).
